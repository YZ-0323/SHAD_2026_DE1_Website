Adaptive · Circular Textiles

Fabric that adapts to you, and gives back to the earth

We replace spandex and elastane with lyocell and natural tree rubber — fibers that stretch to fit the body, hold their shape for years, and fully biodegrade at the end of their life.

Explore the materials How it works →

Two problems, one fiber system

Grows with the body

Lyocell's crystalline structure absorbs 50% more moisture than cotton, breathing and cooling as conditions change.

Shape memory, without spandex

Natural tree rubber recovers its form after stretching, replacing petroleum-based elastane with a renewable alternative.

Built to return to soil

Both fibers biodegrade completely — lyocell within months, latex through natural composting — closing the loop.

The problem

Fast fashion's $400 billion waste problem

Textile consumption has doubled in two decades, yet two-thirds of what's produced ends up in landfill. Most of it is synthetic — fibers that take decades to break down and blends that can't be separated for recycling.

Elastane and spandex make garments stretch, but they're engineered to snap back to one fixed shape and can't be pulled apart from the fabric they're woven into. We build stretch differently, from fibers that flex, absorb, and decompose on their own terms.

From fiber, to wear, to soil

Three stages built into the fabric itself, so the garment's end of life is designed in from the start.

01 — Source

Wood pulp & tree sap

Lyocell is spun from FSC-certified wood pulp in a closed-loop process; natural latex is tapped from trees, not synthesized.

02 — Wear

Stretch, breathe, recover

Moisture-wicking lyocell and elastic natural rubber move with the body and hold their shape through years of use.

03 — Return

Fully biodegradable

At the end of its life, the fabric composts naturally — no microplastics released, no residue left in landfill.
